Molecular diagnostics

Gene detection in modern diagnostics

July 22, 20262 min readBy the BSM team

Advances in molecular diagnostics have transformed healthcare by enabling laboratories to detect diseases at the genetic level: earlier, more precisely and with treatment in mind.

The future of precision medicine

Molecular diagnostics now let laboratories detect disease at the genetic level. For physicians, gene detection means diagnosing conditions earlier, personalising treatments and improving patient outcomes.

What is gene detection?

Gene detection involves analysing DNA or RNA to identify genetic mutations, infectious organisms or inherited disorders. Using techniques such as real-time PCR and other molecular assays, laboratories can provide highly accurate diagnostic information.

Clinical applications

Gene detection is widely used for:

  • Infectious disease diagnosis
  • Cancer biomarker detection
  • Hereditary disease screening
  • Pharmacogenomic testing
  • Prenatal and newborn screening
  • Personalised medicine

Why molecular diagnostics matter

Compared with traditional laboratory methods, molecular diagnostics offer:

  • Detection at the genetic level, in DNA or RNA
  • Highly accurate results from methods such as real-time PCR
  • Early disease detection
  • Targeted treatment planning
  • Precise information to guide clinical decisions

Supporting better patient care

Gene detection gives clinicians precise diagnostic information that leads to faster diagnoses and more effective treatments, from infectious disease and cancer to inherited disorders and pharmacogenomics.
